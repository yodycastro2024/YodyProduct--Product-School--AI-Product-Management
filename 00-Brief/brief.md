# The brief · Volta hiring

## The client
**Volta** — a Series-C climate-tech scaleup, doubling engineering this year (80 → 160 headcount). You're meeting their **VP of Talent**.

## The ask (in her words)
> "We're doubling engineering this year — 80 to 160 — and hiring is the bottleneck for the whole company. The thing that's killing us is our senior engineers are drowning in interviews. Last quarter they spent something like 900 hours on onsites, and more than half of those candidates got rejected _at_ the onsite. So we're flying people in, burning our best ICs for a full day, and it's a no. The recruiters screen first, but honestly the managers don't trust those screens — every manager has a different idea of what 'good' looks like, so a candidate one team loves, another team bounces. Time-to-hire is creeping past 50 days and we've lost two finalists to faster competitors. Oh, and legal is nervous about anything 'AI' touching hiring decisions. Can you help us fix this?"

## The metric to move
- **Primary:** onsite → offer pass rate — currently **~45%** (every failed onsite = wasted senior-engineer time).
- **Secondary:** interviewer-hours-per-hire; time-to-hire (currently **>50 days**).

## What's NOT in the ask
Nobody said "build an AI screener." The first real PM move is **diagnosis**, not building. The root cause here is an **inconsistent, undefined bar** — every manager means something different by "good" — and any AI feature only works _after_ that bar is defined.

## The built-in tension
Legal is nervous about AI in hiring. Treat that as a gift: it forces every governance decision (autonomy level, PII redaction, bias checks, human approval) to be real, not theoretical.
